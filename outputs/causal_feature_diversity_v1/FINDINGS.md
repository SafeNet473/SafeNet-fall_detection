# Recommendation

Keep MCU v1 unchanged. The four-feature model adding gravity-direction change is the strongest compact candidate for a separately validated MCU v2: compared with frozen v1 it recovers 14 additional falls at <=1 FA/hour and five at <=5 FA/hour. Its added information gives material low-alarm improvement under the predeclared rule. This is a validation development result, not a deployment qualification.

All seven features pass the materiality rule versus v1, but recover only two additional falls at <=1 FA/hour and one at <=5 compared with the four-feature orientation model. That incremental low-alarm gain is modest for three further features. The seven-feature model does substantially reduce the >98% sensitivity alarm cost, from 143.09 to 74.64 FA/hour, but that remains far above both engineering targets. Its >=97% alarm cost worsens versus v1 (37.81 versus 24.77 FA/hour); no candidate dominates the entire frontier.

The energy-ratio addition is a cheaper alternative worth considering: one division and small energy accumulators instead of the orientation feature's two square roots, normalization divisions and acos. It improves sensitivity by 1.33 percentage points at <=1 FA/hour and reduces the >=97% alarm cost to 16.62 FA/hour. Orientation change is preferred here only for the declared low-alarm priority, not because it wins every operating regime. Settling variance alone fails the materiality rule. Stop after the six predefined sets.

At <=5 FA/hour, the orientation model changes F13 sensitivity from 80% to 96%, leaves F14 at 100%, and changes F15 from 92% to 96%. The all-seven model reaches 96%, 100%, and 100%, respectively. Orientation change removes the observed D06 and D18 alarms at this operating point and reduces D19's activity-specific rate from 84 to 72/hour. All-seven retains the baseline D06/D18 rates but reduces D19 to 24/hour. These rates use each activity's own short exposure; they are not overall ADL rates.

No set meets >98% sensitivity at <=1 or <=5 FA/hour. At <=1 FA/hour all-seven detects 351/375 falls (93.60%), 17 detections short of the required 368; at <=5 it detects 356/375 (94.93%), 12 short. The fixed trigger misses five falls and caps sensitivity at 370/375 (98.67%). It makes 100% impossible, but does not by itself explain the failure to exceed 98% at low alarm rates: classifier rejections account for the additional gap.

All 24 predefined fits converged. Independent verification checked 56 operating points, exact model/exported-parameter score parity, training-only scaler means, complete score-boundary sweeps, budget optimality, and unchanged authoritative input hashes. No test data were used. Four synthetic feature tests passed. Full equations, state/arithmetic estimates, per-activity results and frontiers accompany REPORT.md. Further gains must be confirmed on independent data, especially real wrist recordings; no candidate is promoted.
